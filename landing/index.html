Hunch

Ask your team a question. See what they actually believe.

Hunch is a Slack bot for thoughtful, anonymous forecasting. Anyone asks a question — a launch date, a hire, a target. Teammates commit play-money coins to YES or NO. The aggregate stays hidden until the question resolves. That's what keeps it honest.

Add to Slack

Free during the beta. Two clicks to install. You'll get a welcome DM.

Why hide the aggregate?

The standard prediction market shows everyone the live consensus. That's a feature in finance and a bug at work. People anchor to the leader's view, conform to the loudest voice, and the signal you wanted — what your team actually believes — gets eaten by the meta-game of guessing what everyone else thinks.

Hunch hides the aggregate until the market resolves. Each person commits their own belief, in coins, in private. The truth shows up at resolution — along with who was right.

What you get

  • Anonymous bets. No one — not your manager, not the question's creator — sees who bet what.
  • Aggregate hidden until resolution. No anchoring. No information cascades.
  • Play money, real signal. Everyone starts with 10,000 coins. Winners take from losers when a market resolves.
  • Workspace admins see the aggregate live — that's the leader's view of what the team really thinks.
  • Two clicks to install. Slash command interface. No accounts to create.

FAQ

Are bets really anonymous?

Yes. The system records who bet what (so we can pay you out when you're right) but no UI surfaces it. Nothing in /hunch admin, the channel card, or the resolution post identifies individual bettors. Only the aggregate.

What does the workspace admin see?

Admins (and the question's creator) see the live aggregate — the team's current YES probability — through /hunch admin. They never see who voted which way. That's the trust boundary the product is built around.

Is this gambling?

No. Hunch uses play money. Coins have no cash value, can't be cashed out, and don't transfer between workspaces.

Where is my data stored?

On Hunch's servers, in Postgres. We store only what's needed to make the bot work: which workspace installed, who placed which bets, and which markets exist. No message content, no DMs, no user emails.

Can I self-host it?

Yes — the source is on GitHub. README has the steps.
